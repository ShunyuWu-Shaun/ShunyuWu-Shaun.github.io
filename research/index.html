Research

Shunyu Wu connects learned physical dynamics to control and optimization across coupled water-energy systems and materials processing.

Learning physical systems for control and optimization

My research in Physical AI for Science examines how learned models represent state evolution under physical constraints and incomplete information. The state may be a temporal quantity such as demand or pressure, or a distributed field defined by initial conditions, geometry, boundary conditions, and external forcing. I evaluate a learned representation through the operating schedule, control action, or physical design computed from it.

Across coupled water-energy systems and materials processing, the same criterion applies: whether a learned representation preserves the physical response that determines the downstream operating decision. Decision-focused forecasting links temporal uncertainty to operating cost, while neural operators map process conditions to spatiotemporal state fields. Control and constrained optimization then test whether those fields retain the rollouts and sensitivities needed for a process decision.

Decision-focused forecasting under uncertainty

Forecast errors matter differently when predictions determine an operating schedule. In coupled water–energy systems, Stochastic Predict-Then-Optimize trains probabilistic electricity-cost forecasts through the stochastic water-supply schedules they induce. A differentiable upper bound on optimality gap passes changes in scheduling cost back to the predictor.

Work on lag-free demand forecasting and uncertain pressure regression examines two additional sources of prediction error. Lag-penalty attention targets delayed reactions near demand turning points, while uncertain regression separates modeled responses to observed inputs from residual pressure variation associated with unobserved disturbances. Their metrics distinguish response timing and disturbance sensitivity from average numerical error.

Neural operators for distributed dynamics

Many control and design problems depend on physical fields that evolve across space and time. I study neural operators that map initial conditions, geometry, boundary conditions, and forcing fields to spatiotemporal solution fields. Their evaluation includes field error, rollout error, and the sensitivities used by control and optimization.

Learning-based control

A learned solution operator can supply state rollouts without solving the governing equations from scratch at every control step. I study how approximation error accumulates over a control horizon and changes the selected action, closed-loop cost, and constraint violation. These quantities provide control-specific criteria for training and evaluating learned physical dynamics.

AI for materials processing

In collaboration with Professor Fengqi You, I develop neural operators that map process conditions, geometry, boundary inputs, and external forcing to evolving material state fields. The resulting rollouts and local sensitivities are used in process control and constrained optimization.

Feasibility, objective value, and decision sensitivity test whether approximation error changes the selected operating condition or physical design. This follows the same criterion as my work on water-energy systems: a learned representation must preserve the physical response that determines the downstream decision.

Related collaborative work

Collaborative work on generated actions examines how learned trajectories satisfy geometric and decision constraints. DRIFT stores local LiDAR geometry as graph memory during recurrent diffusion and evaluates the generated motion through endpoint error, clearance, smoothness, and runtime.

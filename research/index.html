Research

Shunyu Wu connects decision-focused forecasting and constrained optimization with neural operators for distributed physical dynamics, control, and materials processes.

Research program

My research asks how learned models of physical systems can support decisions under dynamics, uncertainty, and constraints. The program began with decision-focused forecasting and constrained optimization for coupled water–energy systems and now extends to neural operators for distributed dynamics, control, and materials processes.

Decision-focused forecasting and constrained optimization

During my doctoral research, I treated a predictor as part of a decision pipeline rather than an endpoint. Stochastic Predict-Then-Optimize trains probabilistic electricity-cost forecasts through the stochastic water-supply schedules they induce. A differentiable upper bound on downstream optimality gap passes a decision-relevant signal back to the predictor.

Companion work on lag-free demand forecasting and uncertain pressure regression addressed when a prediction error occurs and which unobserved effects it represents. Together, these studies shifted evaluation from aggregate forecast accuracy to decision sensitivity, operating cost, and constraint satisfaction.

From predictors to neural operators

Neural operators extend this question from finite-dimensional time series to maps between physical fields. The model class changes, but the research criterion remains the same: a surrogate is useful when it preserves the control or optimization decision that matters.

I study operators that map initial conditions, geometry, boundary conditions, and forcing fields to spatiotemporal solutions. Field error is one diagnostic; rollout stability, gradient quality, control cost, and feasibility determine whether the learned map is suitable for downstream use.

Neural operators in downstream control

A learned solution operator can supply state rollouts to a controller without solving the governing equations from scratch at every control step. I quantify how local approximation error accumulates over a control horizon and changes the selected action. This relation motivates training objectives that account for closed-loop behavior as well as field reconstruction.

Neural operators in constrained optimization

Embedding a differentiable neural operator in an optimization problem links physical field prediction to design or operating decisions. Gradient accuracy and feasibility indicate whether the surrogate can be used inside the optimizer. Decision sensitivity shows when a small field error produces a materially different optimum.

Materials processes as a physical testbed

I am extending this framework to materials processes, where the state evolves as a field over space and time. These systems provide a concrete setting for testing whether learned solution maps can support experiment selection, process control, and constrained design.

Related work on generated actions

A related collaborative thread examines learned action generation in physical environments. DRIFT stores local LiDAR geometry as graph memory during recurrent diffusion, extending the broader interest in models whose outputs must satisfy geometric and decision constraints.

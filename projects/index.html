Research projects

Research projects in Physical AI for Science, focused on physical states, dynamics, uncertainty, control, and constrained optimization.

Physical AI for Science through states, dynamics, and decisions

My research examines how a learned model should represent the state and evolution of a physical system when its output is used in control or optimization. The starting point is the physical quantity to be predicted or reconstructed, together with the inputs, geometry, constraints, and unresolved disturbances that shape its evolution. I then ask which approximation errors alter the resulting schedule, feedback action, trajectory, or design.

Across these projects, the physical system defines admissible states and decisions. Learning estimates a quantity that is uncertain or costly to obtain, and control or optimization converts that estimate into an action. Evaluation includes the error that matters at the decision level, such as optimality gap, operating cost, constraint violation, or trajectory geometry.

Neural operators for materials processing

In collaboration with Professor Fengqi You, I work on AI for materials processing using neural operators. The operator maps process conditions, geometry, boundary inputs, and external forcing to evolving material state fields, then provides rollouts and local sensitivities for control and constrained optimization.

Across materials processing and coupled water-energy systems, I use the same evaluation criterion: whether the learned representation preserves the physical response that determines the schedule, control action, or design.

Research questions and evaluation criteria

Coupled water-energy operation under forecast uncertainty

Urban water treatment, transport, and distribution consume electricity, so uncertain electricity prices can change the preferred operating schedule. SPTO supplies a forecast distribution to a stochastic water-supply model and trains the predictor through a duality-derived upper bound on the schedule’s optimality gap. Operating cost and regret measure whether forecast error changes the induced decision.

Temporal response and uncertain pressure states

Daily demand and network pressure expose different errors in a learned dynamical representation. KbBcM treats delayed reaction near demand turning points as a timing error. CritiCoder separates the pressure response associated with measured inputs from residual variation associated with unobserved disturbances. Their evaluation distinguishes temporal misalignment and disturbance sensitivity from average numerical error.

Feedback laws for constrained nonlinear dynamics

Two last-author studies examine learned feedback laws for nonlinear dynamics. For discrete-time constrained systems, nonlinear model predictive control supplies initial weights for a controller network, while a control barrier function and a nonquadratic loss impose state and input constraints during learning. Related adaptive optimal-control work uses eligibility traces at several time scales so that the critic receives temporal credit over different horizons.

Constrained controller · Multiple time-scale eligibility traces

Multi-step energy dynamics in industrial rolling

Hot-strip roughing unfolds through an ordered sequence of rolling passes in which prediction error can accumulate. A DCLS-Net study models the energy consumption of each pass, while series concatenation propagates information through the rolling order to reduce accumulated multi-step error. The process trajectory, rather than an isolated sample, is the relevant prediction object.

Geometry-conditioned motion in physical environments

DRIFT stores local LiDAR geometry as graph memory and retrieves waypoint-specific context during recurrent diffusion. Endpoint error, clearance, smoothness, and runtime test how the generated trajectory relates to target intent and local spatial structure. This collaborative project asks how an action generator should retain the geometry that constrains motion, a related question in Physical AI.

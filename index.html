RESEARCH

Learning physical systems for control and optimization.

My research asks which physical variables a learned model must represent, how those states evolve under external forcing and incomplete observation, and whether approximation error changes the resulting schedule, control action, or design. Across coupled water-energy systems and AI for materials processing, this question connects decision-focused forecasting with neural operators, learning-based control, and constrained optimization.

Decision-focused forecasting under uncertainty

Probabilistic forecasts are trained and evaluated through the schedules, costs, and constraints they induce. Work on water-demand timing and uncertain pressure regression further identifies when prediction errors occur and which unobserved effects they represent.

Neural operators for distributed dynamics

I study maps from initial conditions, geometry, boundary conditions, and forcing fields to spatiotemporal solutions. Field error, rollout error, and sensitivity error measure how accurately the learned operator represents the dynamics used in control and optimization.

Learning-based control

I examine how approximation error accumulates over a control horizon and changes the selected action, closed-loop cost, and constraint violation. These quantities provide control-specific criteria for training and evaluating learned physical dynamics.

AI for materials processing

In collaboration with Professor Fengqi You, I develop neural operators that map process conditions and boundary inputs to evolving material state fields. Their rollouts and sensitivities support process control and constrained optimization, where feasibility and objective value test whether model error changes the selected decision.

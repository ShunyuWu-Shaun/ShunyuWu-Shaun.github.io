Research

Research by Shunyu Wu on decision-focused learning, time-series forecasting, differentiable optimization, and learning-based decision rules.

Research questions

Forecasts with similar pointwise errors can lead to different schedules or control actions. I study how predictions change decisions when they parameterize an optimizer or rank a candidate action set. Evaluation depends on the application: operating cost and optimality gap for scheduling, demand-trend timing for forecasting, and pressure error for network models.

Decision-focused training for water-energy scheduling

Stochastic Predict-Then-Optimize addresses electricity-cost uncertainty in water-energy resource scheduling. The model combines probabilistic forecasting with a stochastic scheduling problem for urban water supply. Its training objective uses a differentiable convex upper bound on the optimality gap derived from Lagrangian duality, so forecast distributions are evaluated by the schedules they induce.

This line of work examines which forecast errors alter the optimal schedule and how a constrained solver can provide a training signal. It also tests whether lower operating cost or regret comes at the expense of forecast calibration.

Time-series forecasting at turning points

The knowledge-based bi-correction model targets lag in daily urban water-demand forecasts. Lag-penalty attention extracts sequence and feature information associated with timely changes. A stacked LSTM produces the initial forecast, and a masked weighted Markov chain corrects selected residuals using posterior error information.

The same study introduces mean prediction trend effectiveness to test whether a model follows the direction of demand changes more effectively than a naive forecast. This metric complements absolute-error measures because a low average error can conceal a delayed response near a turning point.

Pressure modeling with unobserved disturbances

CritiCoder models macroscopic pressure when measured inputs do not explain all disturbance-related variation. Its Coder estimates the response to observed variables, while the Critic modifies the training loss to represent residual variation as an uncertain disturbance. The study evaluates this separation across monitoring nodes with different exposure to unobserved disturbances.

Explicit selection rules for learned decisions

Two 2026 collaborations extend this interest beyond water systems. GEM uses a Gaussian-mixture actor to retain multiple action hypotheses in offline reinforcement learning. At inference, it ranks a candidate set with an ensemble lower-confidence bound and a behavior-support term standardized within the current state.

DRIFT generates trajectories from local LiDAR observations. Structured Scene Perception stores local geometry as graph memory. During recurrent denoising, the model retrieves waypoint-specific geometry and conditions each update on the target.

Application scope

My first-authored work focuses on urban water demand, pressure modeling, and water-energy scheduling. Collaborative work covers industrial energy-consumption prediction, nonlinear control, offline reinforcement learning, and local-sensing trajectory generation.

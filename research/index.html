Research

Shunyu Wu studies neural operators for physical systems and their use in downstream control, constrained optimization, and materials processes.

Neural operators for physical systems

At Cornell, my postdoctoral research examines neural operators and how their approximation errors propagate into downstream control and constrained optimization. A neural operator learns a map between functions, such as the map from an initial condition and a forcing field to a spatiotemporal solution. I evaluate the learned operator through both field error and the physical decisions it changes.

Materials processes provide one target setting because their state evolves as a field over space and time. Geometry and boundary conditions define the operator input, while measured fields provide training targets. Downstream tests measure the control cost or optimized design produced from the predicted field.

Neural operators in downstream control

A learned solution operator can supply state rollouts to a controller without solving the governing equations from scratch at every control step. My research studies how local approximation error accumulates over a control horizon and changes the selected action. This relation motivates training objectives that account for closed-loop behavior as well as field reconstruction.

Neural operators in constrained optimization

Embedding a differentiable neural operator in an optimization problem links physical field prediction to design or operating decisions. Gradient accuracy and feasibility indicate whether the surrogate can be used inside the optimizer. Decision sensitivity shows when a small field error produces a materially different optimum.

Physical AI for materials processes

Materials processing often requires a map from process conditions and geometry to a field that evolves over space and time. I use neural operators to approximate this map across operating conditions. The predicted field then parameterizes experiment selection, process control, or constrained design.

Decision-focused learning

Stochastic Predict-Then-Optimize trains a probabilistic electricity-cost forecast against the stochastic water-supply schedule that follows. A differentiable convex upper bound on optimality gap, derived from Lagrangian duality, passes a decision-relevant training signal back to the predictor.

This formulation distinguishes prediction errors that change the selected schedule from errors that leave the decision unchanged. The same principle motivates operator-learning objectives that weight field errors by their effect on a controller or optimizer.

Scientific forecasting under uncertainty

The knowledge-based bi-correction model targets delayed reactions near demand turning points through lag-penalty attention and posterior residual correction. CritiCoder separates the response to measured inputs from pressure variation associated with unobserved disturbances.

These studies provide tools for scientific time series in which timing, uncertainty, and unmeasured effects determine the value of a prediction. The relevant evaluation quantity depends on the downstream scientific action rather than a single aggregate error score.

Generative decisions in physical environments

DRIFT stores local LiDAR geometry as graph memory. During recurrent diffusion denoising, the model retrieves waypoint-specific context and coordinates the trajectory with a target. This work connects generative modeling with local physical structure and explicit action generation.
